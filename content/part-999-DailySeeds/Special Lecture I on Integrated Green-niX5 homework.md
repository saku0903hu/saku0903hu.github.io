	In this class, I learned about the remarkable 50-year history of CMOS scaling and the critical physical limitations it currently faces. As conventional device scaling approaches its limits, engineers must overcome significant hurdles to continue improving power, performance, area, and cost metrics, often referred to as PPAC. What attracted my attention the most is how the semiconductor industry has systematically tackled two major challenges: the Short Channel Effect (SCE) and parasitic resistance. As the channel length decreases, it becomes fundamentally harder to turn off the field-effect transistor (FET), leading to increased leakage current and a degraded sub-threshold slope, a phenomenon known as SCE. Additionally, as devices shrink further, external parasitic resistance—particularly contact resistance (Rc​)—starts to dominate the overall transistor resistance, heavily hindering performance improvements.

	To suppress these severe issues, transistor architecture has undergone significant evolutionary steps. When conventional planar transistors could no longer effectively control SCE, the industry transitioned to the FinFET architecture. By utilizing a three-dimensional fin structure, FinFETs provided stronger gate control over the channel, enabling further scaling of the channel length while strictly keeping leakage current under control. Furthermore, taller fins allowed for an increased effective channel width per footprint. However, at extremely scaled gate pitches, even FinFETs face electrostatic limitations. This bottleneck led to the introduction of the Gate-All-Around (GAA) Nanosheet architecture. By completely surrounding the silicon channel with the gate, Nanosheets offer superior electrostatics and much better SCE control. Additionally, they provide excellent design flexibility, as the nanosheet width can be freely adjusted to optimize for power and performance, successfully bypassing the rigid quantization limits of FinFETs.

	In conclusion, it was absolutely fascinating to learn that simply shrinking physical dimensions is no longer a viable path for historical CMOS scaling. Instead, continuous architectural innovations—from Planar to FinFET and now to GAA Nanosheet—are the ultimate key enablers that overcome critical physical barriers like SCE and parasitic resistance. Understanding how these structural changes directly address fundamental physics problems gave me a deeper appreciation for the future of advanced logic device technology.
