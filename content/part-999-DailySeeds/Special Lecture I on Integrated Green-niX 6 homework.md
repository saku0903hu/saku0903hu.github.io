**Report Assignment 1: Fundamentals and Evolution of Image Sensors**

In the first part of the lecture covering the fundamentals and evolution of image sensors, the technology that left the strongest impression on me was the **Back-illuminated CMOS Image Sensor**. According to the lecture, the continuous evolution of CMOS image sensors has led to significant improvements in key performance indicators such as resolution and signal-to-noise (SN) ratio. The introduction of the back-illuminated structure fundamentally improved light utilization efficiency. As explained in the lecture's context of sensor evolution, this breakthrough made it possible to **simultaneously achieve pixel miniaturization for higher resolution and high sensitivity**. Beyond the technical explanations, I imagine the most profound ripple effect of this technology has been the **democratization of high-quality visual expression and the explosive spread of visual communication**. By enabling microscopic smartphone camera modules to capture high-definition photos and clear, low-noise images even in dark environments, this technology empowered ordinary users to beautifully capture and share their daily lives on mobile devices. Consequently, this technological leap not only drove the continuous expansion of the mobile market but also dramatically increased the volume of visual content produced by the general public, ultimately serving as the driving force behind a new global culture of enjoying pictures and videos.

**Report Assignment 2: Further Evolution of Image Sensors**

Regarding the further evolution of image sensors discussed in the second half of the lecture, the initiative that impressed me the most was the **development of the Event-based Vision Sensor (EVS) and its integration with AI**. Unlike conventional frame-based image sensors, EVS detects only changes in luminance (events) at each pixel asynchronously along the time axis, which enables **low-power, high-resolution data capture and extremely smooth video frame interpolation**. As this technology fuses with AI, I predict a significant shift in the core value provided to users: the primary "consumer" of the sensor's data will expand **from humans to machines**. Historically, imaging technology has focused on providing beautiful images to evoke emotion in the human eye. However, the future points toward "sensing," which adds value to data by capturing spatial depth (3D), temporal changes (4D), and invisible spectral light for AI to process. Consequently, systems like autonomous vehicles, smart factory robots, and smart city infrastructure will use this highly accurate object recognition to make instantaneous decisions. Ultimately, the human user base will evolve from being merely an audience viewing screens to becoming the **beneficiaries of a safer, more efficient autonomous society** orchestrated by the seamless collaboration of advanced sensing technologies and AI.
