**1. Feature of silicon wafer industry and market** 
    Silicon wafers are essential materials utilized in semiconductor devices across many areas of society. The silicon wafer market continues to grow alongside the semiconductor market, showing a higher compound annual growth rate (CAGR of ~5.8% from 1990 to 2024) compared to other material industries like steel or copper. A key characteristic of the industry is that the top 5 silicon wafer makers ("Big 5") fabricate almost all of the silicon wafers in the world, with Japanese companies maintaining a high market share of around 45% to 60%. The driving force for the future silicon wafer market is expected to be data servers, smartphones, and automotive applications. With the continuous miniaturization of semiconductor devices for cost reduction, wafer diameters have become larger, and 300mm silicon wafers are currently the main product.

**2. Difference of silicon single crystal growth method** 
    There are three main methods for growing silicon single crystals: CZ (Czochralski), MCZ (Magnetic Czochralski), and FZ (Floating Zone) methods.

- **CZ method:** A seed crystal is dipped into silicon melt and then pulled while rotating. This is the typical method for silicon wafers, capable of producing large ingots with a maximum diameter of 300mm. However, because it uses a quartz crucible, it incorporates a high amount of oxygen (~1-2x10^18 atoms/cm³).
- **MCZ method:** This method is almost the same as the CZ method, except that a magnetic field is applied to the silicon melt. It can also produce large ingots up to 300mm in diameter, but the applied magnetic field allows for a moderate amount of oxygen incorporation (10^17 atoms/cm³ order).
- **FZ method:** Polysilicon is melted by high-frequency induction heating to grow a single crystal. Since it does not use a crucible, the oxygen incorporation is very low (1x10^15 - 1x10^16 atoms/cm³). However, the maximum diameter is limited to 200mm, resulting in middle-sized ingots.

**3. Quality items of silicon single crystal ingot by CZ method** 
    There are five main quality items manufactured during the crystal growth by the CZ method:

1. **Shape (diameter):** The wafer diameter is controlled by directly monitoring the single crystal pulling with a camera.
2. **Dislocation free:** Using the "Dash neck method", the seed diameter is squeezed so that dislocations generated by thermal shock terminate at the outside of the crystal.
3. **Desired resistivity:** Resistivity depends on the segregation of the dopant (like Boron or Phosphorus) concentration. The dopant concentration incorporated in the first half and the second half of the crystal is different due to segregation as the crystal pulling proceeds.
4. **Moderate oxygen concentration:** Oxygen is incorporated from the quartz crucible through melting, convection, and evaporation. Oxygen is a "double-edged sword" because oxygen precipitates improve mechanical strength and provide gettering for metal contamination, but they can also cause degradation of device properties.
5. **Crystal defects:** Controlling grown-in defects such as Voids (COP), OSF (Oxidation-induced Stacking Fault), Dislocation clusters, and BMDs.

**4. Detail of control for grown-in defect in silicon single crystal during crystal pulling process** 
    Grown-in defects like voids, OSFs, and dislocation clusters are the origin of device failures such as oxide breakdown voltage failure and PN junction leaks. Therefore, crystal perfection is necessary to suppress these failures. These defects can be suppressed and a defect-free silicon wafer can be obtained by precisely controlling the **"V/G"** ratio, where "V" is the crystal pulling rate and "G" is the temperature gradient between the single crystal and the melt. By optimizing the V/G value, the thermal history is controlled to manage the density and size of point defects (vacancies and interstitial silicon). Today, numerical simulation technology is a powerful tool used to predict and optimize the defect regions inside the silicon ingot to successfully obtain defect-free blocks of silicon.
